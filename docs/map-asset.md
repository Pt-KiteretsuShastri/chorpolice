# Painted map asset

`dist/assets/neighbourhood.webp` is the actual playable background, derived from the user's supplied `image(4).png`. The background preserves the supplied 1536 × 1024 street layout and detailed illustrated graphics. It is mapped to the game's 1472 × 960 world, widened from 1280 × 832 without shrinking characters. The image is bundled locally, not hotlinked.

The built-in image-generation editor removed baked-in gameplay content. Its final prompt was:

> Use case: precise-object-edit. Asset type: production background map for a top-down browser chase game. Edit the supplied image, preserving its exact 1536x1024 composition, camera angle, warm detailed hand-painted graphics, buildings, streets and roof placement. This is the actual playable map, not a new concept. Remove only the following baked-in gameplay elements and restore the underlying road, paving or roof texture seamlessly: the running thief and pressure cooker at centre-left; the running police character on centre-right street; both white speech bubbles and their text; the entire mint green dotted route and arrowheads; the samosa character on the bazaar counter; the golden trophy on the upper-right house rooftop; the radio object on the lower-centre yellow rooftop. Also remove the blue-and-white police jeep parked outside CHOWKI and the green/yellow auto-rickshaw in the upper central lane, restoring paving so animated vehicles can be drawn there. Keep the vendors, buildings, small stationary neighbours, safehouse, park, banyan tree, roof terraces, clothes, solar panels, water tanks, parked scooters, bunting, flowers, plants and decorative details. Keep the BAZAAR, CHOWKI, SAFEHOUSE and CHAI signs and their exact positions. Do not add new objects, people, labels, UI, paths, loot or icons. Preserve the existing street network and roof outlines exactly. Final image must be the same fully filled rectangular scene without borders, ideally 1536x1024, detailed and crisp.

The result was encoded as WebP at quality 88. `world.js` authors collision polygons, roof rectangles, gates, access points, candidate loot locations and route waypoints against the supplied image coordinates. `P(x, y)` converts those reference coordinates into world coordinates. Navigation uses 8 px nodes to fit curved lanes; collision is continuous circle-versus-polygon and never uses image colours. Diagonal navigation edges sample clearance to prevent cutting solid corners.

To change the illustration, update the geometry explicitly, revalidate all objectives and entrances, and inspect an overlay before committing. Static vendors and rooftop props are decorative. Dynamic chor, cop, treasures, route, walkers and cars are rendered by the game.
